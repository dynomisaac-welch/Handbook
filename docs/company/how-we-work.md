# How We Work

This page describes our operating norms — how we make decisions, communicate, run projects, and improve over time. It's written for anyone joining the team or trying to understand how we operate day to day.

---

## Decision-Making

We operate on **autonomy with accountability**. Each leader and team member owns decisions within their domain and is accountable for the outcomes. The expectation is to act — not to seek permission at every turn.

A few principles that guide how we decide:

**Don't escalate decisions — escalate conflicts.** If a decision falls within your domain, make it. If it genuinely crosses into another domain, align with that owner. Only escalate when alignment fails.

**Disagree and commit.** We debate openly and assume good intent. Once a decision is made, everyone commits — even those who argued the other side. Relitigating settled decisions erodes trust and momentum.

**Oppose with a proposal.** If you challenge an idea, come with an alternative. "That won't work" isn't a contribution. "That won't work, and here's what I'd do instead" is.

**Document divergence.** If you make a decision that deviates from established practice, write down why. Patterns of undocumented divergence mean the documented approach needs updating.

## Communication

We keep communication lean and purposeful. The goal is clarity and speed, not volume.

**Channels:**
- **Teams** — quick questions, status updates, FYIs. Expected response within a few hours during working hours.
- **Email** — formal communication, client-facing content, documentation. Respond within 24 hours.
- **Meeting** — real-time discussion, decisions, unblocking. Scheduled in advance with an agenda.
- **ADO** — story-specific discussion, technical detail, async decisions on work items.
- **Phone/video (urgent)** — blockers, critical issues, escalations. Immediate during working hours.

**After hours:** Not expected. If something is genuinely urgent, call or text — not Slack or email.

**Meeting norms:** Start on time, end on time. Come prepared. Cameras on by default. Close distractions. Every meeting ends with clear next steps and named owners. If a meeting doesn't need to happen, cancel it — a well-written message is often better.

## How We Run Projects

We use **Practical Agile** — structured enough to maintain predictability and quality, flexible enough to adapt to client realities. We don't run identical ceremonies on every engagement, but we hold to the underlying discipline.

**The baseline on every project:**
- A defined sprint cadence (typically two weeks)
- A sprint goal, agreed at planning
- A daily standup: what I did, what I'm doing, what's blocking me
- A demo at sprint end — only completed work, in a production-like environment
- A retrospective — internal, psychologically safe, focused on process not people
- A backlog kept groomed and ready

**Definition of Ready:** A story enters a sprint only when acceptance criteria are clear and testable, it's estimated, and dependencies are resolved. We don't pull half-formed work into sprints.

**Definition of Done:** Code reviewed and approved, tests passing, QA complete, deployed to staging, Product Owner accepted. Done means done — not "mostly done" or "waiting on review."

## How We Handle Conflict

Conflict is a sign the team is engaging. Avoidance is the real problem.

**Step 1 — Go direct.** Address the issue with the person involved. Assume positive intent. Focus on behavior and impact, not character. Use "I" statements.

**Step 2 — Bring in a mediator.** If a direct conversation doesn't resolve it, involve a delivery manager or team lead to facilitate — not to decide, but to help both parties reach understanding.

**Step 3 — Escalate.** If still unresolved, escalate to the portfolio manager or VP of Delivery for formal resolution.

What's not okay: passive-aggressive behavior, talking behind someone's back, personal attacks, or ignoring the issue and hoping it disappears.

## How We Improve

We treat our processes the same way we treat software — ship, learn, iterate.

Every project team holds retrospectives. Every quarter, delivery and engineering leadership review the operating model. When something isn't working, we name it, document the change, and move on. We don't preserve bad processes out of habit.

**This handbook is a living document.** If something here conflicts with how work is actually happening — and that divergence is intentional — update the handbook. If the divergence isn't intentional, fix the practice.

Anyone on the team can propose a change. The standard is: bring a proposal, not just a complaint.
